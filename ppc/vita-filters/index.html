Weekly performance, January to August 2026

5 January – 16 August 2026 · complete weeks only (Monday–Sunday) · Google Ads & Microsoft Ads

Why the two most recent weeks look lower than they will end up

A purchase is credited back to the day the shopper clicked the ad, not the day they paid. So the newest weeks are always still filling in — pool equipment is a considered purchase, and the bigger the basket, the longer the gap between the click and the order.

In the revenue and ROAS charts below, the solid part of each bar is what the account reports today; the shaded part is what we expect to still land, based on each network’s own measured purchase-lag curve. That shaded portion shrinks as a week ages, which is why the older weeks are effectively final.

The curves behind the shaded band were re-measured on 21 August against 16 days of realised fill-in, and both came out flatter than the earlier estimate — so the projections here are more conservative than the ones shown on 5 August. Google weeks more than two months old moved by less than 0.7% over those 16 days, and April and earlier did not move at all. One caveat on Microsoft: its fill-in is lumpy rather than even. The week of 27 July reported $36.2k on 5 August and now reports $51.1k, a 41% gain that came almost entirely from the branded search campaign. Read the Microsoft shaded band as a floor, not a ceiling.

The two networks fill in at different rates, so each is projected on its own curve. In Google Ads, about 70% of revenue lands the same day and the rest trails in over the following days and weeks — most of it inside the first month, after which a week barely moves. Microsoft Ads settles faster: roughly 10% more revenue arrives after a week closes and it is essentially done within two weeks.

Revenue

The last two weeks are the ones still filling in materially: the week of 3 August is about 92% complete and the week of 10 August about 88%. Both August weeks earned more revenue per dollar than the week of 27 July did, on lower spend.

Return on ad spend

Because spend is already final, the projected revenue divides into the same spend — so the shaded portion carries straight through to ROAS. The dashed line marks the 8:1 target. Ad spend

Final for every week. Microsoft investment came down alongside Google through July and has since held flat at about $3.2–3.3k a week — roughly a fifth the size of the Google budget.

Revenue

Only the two newest weeks carry a shaded segment: Microsoft fills in about 10% more after a week closes, so anything older than two weeks is already final here. The swings in this account are real rather than an artefact of filling in — 29 June returned $19.0k and 6 July returned $42.9k on almost identical spend. The week of 27 July is the standout at $51.1k, the best week since 25 May on less than half the spend.

Return on ad spend

Same 8:1 target line and the same scale as Google Ads, so the two networks can be read against each other. Microsoft is running well clear of the target: 1,535% in the week of 27 July, 1,367% in the week of 3 August, and 996% reported so far for the week of 10 August, which projects to about 1,106%. That is the first run of three consecutive weeks above target since March.
